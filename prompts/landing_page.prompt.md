## Objective

QA's job is changing, not just its tools. The goal is direct B2B bookings via a strategy-call form.


## Strict Rules & Terminology
1. **Banned words:** never use "course," "training," "workshop," or "class." Use B2B terms instead: *Transformation Program, AI-Augmented QA Playbook, Upskilling Framework, Enablement Initiative.*
2. **Bottleneck warning:** state plainly that AI lets developers ship faster, and if QA doesn't change too, testing becomes the bottleneck that erases those gains.
3. **Mindset shift:** frame the change as moving from manual/scripted execution to a creative, strategic, adaptive AI-driven testing practice — and specifically from SDLC to ADLC.
4. **Concrete AI value:** show real, specific outputs (not adjectives) — automated test-case synthesis, smart edge-case generation, instant root-cause analysis — with actual example code/scenarios.
5. **Multi-LLM stack:** show that the offering uses multiple real models — GitHub Copilot, Claude, Gemini — each doing a *different, named job*, with their real logos linked from a public icon CDN (e.g., `[https://cdn.simpleicons.org/[slug](https://cdn.simpleicons.org/[slug)]`, slugs: `githubcopilot`, `claude`, `googlegemini`), with a note that these marks belong to their respective owners.
6. **Domain focus:** scope everything to e-commerce QA specifically — cart & checkout, payments & fraud rules, catalog & search, inventory & fulfillment, promotions & pricing, peak-traffic regression — not generic software testing.
7. **Plain language:** keep sentences short and concrete. Define SDLC and ADLC in one line each the first time they appear. Avoid stacking multiple claims into one sentence.
8. **Title/positioning:** the page's core identity is "QA Engineering with AI."

## Value Proposition

- **Show how the traditional SDLC is evolving into an AI-Driven Development Lifecycle (ADLC).**  
  Software development roles are increasingly moving toward an **AI Builder model**, where professionals use AI to design, develop, test, optimize, and maintain software within their area of expertise. Java Developers, Python Developers, QA Engineers, DevOps Engineers, and other technology professionals will increasingly combine their domain expertise with AI capabilities. For example, a QA Engineer evolves into an **AI Builder specialized in Quality Engineering and Quality Assurance**.

- **Prepare IT professionals for the scale and speed of AI-generated software.**  
  AI enables businesses to generate and modify thousands of lines of code in a fraction of the time previously required. This creates a new challenge: producing code is becoming easier, while ensuring its **quality, reliability, security, maintainability, and alignment with business requirements** becomes increasingly critical.  
  An AI Builder with a strong Quality Engineering background must be able to identify the most critical risks, validate AI-generated solutions, and rapidly adapt software to changing business requirements.

- **Demonstrate that AI proficiency is becoming a core capability for technology professionals.**  
  AI tools will increasingly become part of everyday software development. Professionals who fail to integrate AI into their workflows risk falling behind as development practices, productivity expectations, and required competencies continue to evolve.

- **Emphasize that simply using AI will not be enough.**  
  As AI adoption becomes widespread, access to AI tools will no longer be a differentiator. The professionals who stand out will be those who develop **reliable, practical, and repeatable AI-driven engineering skills** and understand how to apply AI effectively within real software delivery environments.

- **Position holistic technical understanding as a major business advantage.**  
  Professionals who understand software architecture, development, testing, infrastructure, and business requirements from an end-to-end perspective become significantly more valuable to organizations. When you understand the technology, know how to leverage AI, and can evaluate software quality holistically, you are able not only to execute tasks but also to **propose solutions, identify risks, challenge assumptions, and support better technical and business decisions**.

- **Position Quality Engineering as a foundational competence for every AI Builder.**  
  The course should clearly communicate that it is not designed exclusively for QA Engineers. It is intended for **all IT professionals who want to build strong Quality Engineering capabilities in an AI-driven development environment**.  
  Whether you are a Developer, DevOps Engineer, Automation Engineer, Software Engineer, or already working as an AI Builder, understanding how to assess and improve software quality will become increasingly important.

- **Core message for the target audience:**  
  **If you are already building software with AI but do not have a strong Quality Engineering background, this course is designed for you.** It will help you develop the skills required to validate AI-generated software, identify critical risks, design effective testing strategies, and build reliable solutions at AI speed.
