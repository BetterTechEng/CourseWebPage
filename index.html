01 / Why now

AI builds software faster.
Your QA capability has to keep pace.

SDLC — Software Development Lifecycle: people plan, build and test in stages.

ADLC — Agentic Development Lifecycle: agentic AI builds, tests and fixes code in a continuous loop while people direct, review and decide.

More generated code means more decisions about trust. Quality is no longer a final checkpoint. Continuous quality is what makes AI speed usable for the business.

The New Software Development Lifecycle Model

02 / Mindset shift

Learning AI tools is the easy part.
Changing how you work is what counts.

AI becomes part of everyday development and quality work, not an occasional add-on. That needs a new mindset as well as new skills.

03 / New responsibilities

The boundaries of QA are blurring.
Quality ownership is not.

AI lets developers generate tests, analysts validate requirements and QA engineers write code with agents. This cohort prepares you to own those decisions — backed by evidence — whatever else your role on the team includes.

05 / The case

Practise on a real app,
not on slides.

The scenario

Lumeo launches a new sales platform in eight weeks, and its current way of looking after quality can’t keep up with the pace of change. You join as a quality engineer. Your mission: design an AI-based quality process from scratch and run it in Lumeo’s systems before launch day.

UI

Web store

Catalog, product page, cart, checkout and order history. Stable test IDs for automation.

API

REST API

OpenAPI docs at /api/docs, token-based login, error codes and an X-Request-Id on every response for tracing.

DB

SQLite database

Query orders, stock and payments directly — for example with DB Browser for SQLite — and check what the UI does not show.

What you will test

  • Login with fictional standard, VIP and blocked users
  • Catalog search, filters, sorting and stock levels
  • Product variants and quantity limits
  • Cart totals and VAT
  • Percentage and fixed-amount discount codes with their rules
  • Free shipping above a threshold
  • Checkout with address validation and shipping methods
  • Mock payments: success, decline and timeout

What comes with it

  • A requirements document for the new cart and payments, with user stories and acceptance criteria — and the gaps real requirements have
  • Version 1 and version 2, with release notes for the new version
  • One-command start: npm install && npm start
  • GitHub Codespaces as a ready backup environment
  • Fictional data only: users, products and test cards

07 / Artificial Intelligence models

AI skills, taught exactly
where you need them.

The AI Verification Loop

  1. Context
  2. Prompt
  3. AI output
  4. Verification
  5. Human decision
  6. Documented
  • AI drafts, you decide. Responsibility for quality stays with you.
  • Every AI claim needs evidence: a line in the requirements, an API response, a log or a test result.
  • Don’t paste anything you wouldn’t post on a public forum.
Stages 0–2

Models, process & requirements

  • Human-in-the-loop and accountability
  • Prompting for QA: role, context, task, output format, constraints
  • Context: what and how much to give a model
  • Hallucinations: requirements, endpoints and APIs that don’t exist
  • Overconfidence and sycophancy
Stages 3–4

Risks, tests & data

  • The illusion of coverage in AI-generated tests
  • The oracle problem and AI arithmetic errors
  • Test data from AI that only looks valid
  • Security, confidentiality and prompt injection
Stages 5–6

Automation & repeatability

  • AI-generated test code: weak assertions, fixed waits, brittle locators
  • AI agents in the IDE: autonomy levels and reviewing every diff
  • Non-determinism: why answers differ and how to get a repeatable result
Stages 7–8

Process & launch

  • AI-written bug reports: invented steps and guesses as facts
  • Root-cause analysis with AI: hypotheses tested against evidence
  • Reporting and release decisions without invented metrics
  • Evaluating AI output with rubrics and mini-evals

09 / Who it’s for

Is this cohort right for you?

A strong fit if you…

  • Have experience in IT projects, for example in testing, system analysis or requirements analysis.
  • Want to build universal, AI-based quality skills that you can apply in any project.
  • Don’t need to know how to code or to work in quality today — neither is required.
  • Are ready to work actively: 4–6 hours a week, in live meetings and on your own.

Not the right fit if you…

  • Have no experience in IT projects yet.
  • Want a certificate or exam preparation.
  • Only want a list of prompts or a tour of AI tools.
  • Expect AI to replace human judgment and review.

What you need

  • An account with at least one AI assistant: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot. A free plan is enough; a paid plan is recommended.
  • A laptop where you can install software: Chrome or Edge, VS Code, Node.js LTS and Git. A setup guide is provided. Alternative: GitHub Codespaces.

What we ask of you

  • Your employer’s consent to use AI. On the course you work only with fictional data.
  • Openness to changing how you work and time for 4–6 hours a week over eight weeks.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a paid AI subscription?

No. A free plan of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or GitHub Copilot is enough for every challenge. A paid plan is recommended because it gives you longer conversations and fewer limits.

What hardware and software do I need?

A laptop where you can install software, with Chrome or Edge, VS Code, Node.js LTS and Git. The setup guide walks you through it, and on Day zero you check that Lumeo runs before the first stage. If a local install is not possible, you can use GitHub Codespaces in the browser.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. You don’t need to be able to program or to work in quality today. What you do need is experience in IT projects — for example in testing, system analysis or requirements analysis. AI helps with the technical parts, and you review the results.

How much time does it take?

Eight weeks with two live meetings a week and your own work in Lumeo’s systems — 4–6 hours a week in total, plus a short onboarding on Day zero. Meeting days and times will be announced with the dates.

Do I get a certificate?

No. You get eight working solutions and a described process as proof of your competence, with feedback on your work. Micro-credentials may be offered in future cohorts, but they are not part of the current offer.

Will I use my company’s data or code?

No. Everything happens in Lumeo with fictional users, products and payment data. You should still have your employer’s consent to use AI tools.

What if I miss a meeting?

Meetings are recorded with participants’ consent, and recordings stay available for 30 days. The Workbook after each stage includes reference solutions so you can catch up.

Which language is the cohort in?

English and Polish cohorts run separately. The Lumeo app and its requirements document are in English for everyone.

Can my employer pay?

Yes. The cohort is for individual participants, and either you or your employer can pay. Payment details will be published with the price.
