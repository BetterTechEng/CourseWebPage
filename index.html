01 / Why now

AI builds software faster.
Your QA capability has to keep pace.

SDLC — Software Development Lifecycle: people plan, build and test in stages.

ADLC — AI Development Lifecycle: agentic AI builds, tests and fixes code in a continuous loop while people direct, review and decide.

More generated code means more decisions about trust. Quality is no longer a final checkpoint. Continuous quality is what makes AI speed usable for the business.

SDLC — Sequential delivery → ADLC — Continuous quality

AI speed in development needs AI speed in QA — and QA specialists who know when not to trust the AI.
02 / Mindset shift

Learning AI tools is the easy part.
Changing how you work is what counts.

AI becomes part of everyday development and quality work, not an occasional add-on. That needs a new mindset as well as new skills.

03 / Quality ownership

The boundaries of QA are blurring.
Quality ownership is not.

QA, development and business analysis increasingly overlap. Someone still has to own the answer to “can we ship this?”

AI lets developers generate tests, analysts validate requirements and QA engineers write code with agents. This cohort prepares you to own those decisions — backed by evidence — whatever else your role on the team includes.

04 / Learning outcomes

What you will be able to do.

Twelve measurable learning outcomes, each checked on what you produce — not on a quiz. Across all of them: the AI Verification Loop, so you catch AI mistakes before they reach a release.

  • ✓

    Challenge requirements with AI.

    Find ambiguities and contradictions in a product requirements document, reject hallucinated requirements and build a ranked risk register.

  • ✓

    Design risk-based tests.

    Apply test design techniques, spot the coverage gaps in an AI-generated suite and verify test data and expected values.

  • ✓

    Explore the UI, API and database.

    Run AI-assisted exploratory sessions, call the API directly and verify what really landed in the database.

  • ✓

    Write bug reports developers trust.

    Report only what you observed and can prove — even when AI helps you write it.

  • ✓

    Review and automate.

    Review AI-generated test code, automate critical risks with Playwright and show that your tests fail when the product is broken.

  • ✓

    Diagnose and decide.

    Tell regressions, flaky tests, test defects and environment issues apart with evidence, then back a go/no-go recommendation with data.

05 / The case

Practise on a real app,
not on slides.

ShopLite is a small web store built for this course. It runs on your laptop and behaves like a real product — including defects for you to find.

The scenario

ShopLite is launching a new checkout with discount codes and free shipping before Black Friday. You are the only QA on the team, and the release is in two weeks.

UI

Web store

Catalog, product page, cart, checkout and order history. Stable test IDs for automation.

API

REST API

OpenAPI docs at /api/docs, token-based login, error codes and an X-Request-Id on every response for tracing.

DB

SQLite database

Query orders, stock and payments directly — for example with DB Browser for SQLite — and check what the UI does not show.

What you will test

  • Login with fictional standard, VIP and blocked users
  • Catalog search, filters, sorting and stock levels
  • Product variants and quantity limits
  • Cart totals and VAT
  • Percentage and fixed-amount discount codes with their rules
  • Free shipping above a threshold
  • Checkout with address validation and shipping methods
  • Mock payments: success, decline and timeout

What comes with it

  • A product requirements document with user stories and acceptance criteria — and the gaps real requirements have
  • Version 1 and version 2, with release notes for the new version
  • One-command start: npm install && npm start
  • GitHub Codespaces as a ready backup environment
  • Fictional data only: users, products and test cards

07 / AI topics

AI skills, taught exactly
where you need them.

Sixteen AI-specific topics, each introduced just in time in a short “Theory in Brief” and practised on AI output that contains deliberate mistakes.

The AI Verification Loop

  1. Context
  2. Prompt
  3. AI output
  4. Verification
  5. Human decision
  6. Documented
  • AI drafts, you decide. Responsibility for quality stays with you.
  • Every AI claim needs evidence: a line in the requirements, an API response, a log or a test result.
  • Don’t paste anything you wouldn’t post on a public forum.
Session 1

Requirements, risks & test design

  • Human-in-the-loop and accountability
  • Prompting for QA: role, context, task, output format, constraints
  • Context: what and how much to give a model
  • Hallucinations: requirements, endpoints and APIs that don’t exist
  • Overconfidence and sycophancy
  • Non-determinism: comparing different AI answers
  • The illusion of coverage in AI-generated tests
Session 2

Test execution

  • The oracle problem and AI arithmetic errors
  • Test data from AI that only looks valid
  • AI-written bug reports: invented steps and guesses as facts
  • Security, confidentiality and prompt injection
Session 3

Automation

  • AI-generated test code: weak assertions, fixed waits, brittle locators
  • AI agents in the IDE: autonomy levels and reviewing every diff
Session 4

Troubleshooting & reporting

  • Root-cause analysis with AI: hypotheses tested against evidence
  • Reporting and release decisions without invented metrics
  • Evaluating AI output with rubrics and mini-evals

09 / Who it’s for

Is this cohort right for you?

A strong fit if you…

  • Work as a manual or automation tester, at least at junior level (around 6 months of commercial experience).
  • Use AI now and then, but without a systematic approach.
  • Want to use AI across the whole QA flow — and know when not to trust it.
  • Are ready to work actively in exercises, on your own and in pairs.

Not the right fit if you…

  • Are new to testing and have not worked on a QA team yet.
  • Want a certificate or exam preparation.
  • Only want a list of prompts or a tour of AI tools.
  • Expect AI to replace QA judgment and review.

Two paths, one set of challenges

Path M · Manual

You know test cases, bug reporting and basic test design techniques. In automation you build a test with AI, with the focus on reviewing and understanding the code.

Path A · Automation

As path M, plus basic JavaScript/TypeScript, Java or Python and any UI framework such as Playwright, Selenium or Cypress. You also get API, Page Object and concurrency stretch tasks.

You choose your path when you sign up and can change it during the cohort.

What you need

  • An account with at least one AI assistant: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot. A free plan is enough; a paid plan is recommended.
  • A laptop where you can install software: Chrome or Edge, VS Code, Node.js LTS and Git. A setup guide is provided. Alternative: GitHub Codespaces.
  • Your employer’s consent to use AI. On the course you work only with fictional data.
  • Openness to changing how you work and time for about 2 hours of work between sessions.

13 / Questions

FAQ

Do I need a paid AI subscription?

No. A free plan of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or GitHub Copilot is enough for every challenge. A paid plan is recommended because it gives you longer conversations and fewer limits.

What hardware and software do I need?

A laptop where you can install software, with Chrome or Edge, VS Code, Node.js LTS and Git. The setup guide walks you through it, and on Day 0 you check that ShopLite runs before the first session. If a local install is not possible, you can use GitHub Codespaces in the browser.

I’m a manual tester. Can I keep up with the automation session?

Yes. Path M builds a test with AI and focuses on reviewing and understanding the code. Path A adds API, Page Object and concurrency tasks. The challenges are the same for everyone; only the stretch tasks differ.

How much time does it take?

Four live sessions of 3 hours on Tuesdays and Thursdays over two weeks, about 2 hours of work between sessions and a short onboarding before the start — around 18–20 hours in total. Session times will be announced with the dates.

Do I get a certificate?

No. You get a portfolio of artefacts and feedback on them against twelve learning outcomes. Micro-credentials may be offered in future cohorts, but they are not part of the current offer.

Will I use my company’s data or code?

No. Everything happens on ShopLite with fictional users, products and payment data. You should still have your employer’s consent to use AI tools.

What if I miss a session?

Sessions are recorded with participants’ consent, and recordings stay available for 30 days. The Workbook after each session includes reference solutions so you can catch up.

Which language is the cohort in?

English and Polish cohorts run separately. The ShopLite app and its requirements document are in English for everyone.

Can my employer pay?

Yes. The cohort is for individual QA specialists, and either you or your employer can pay. Payment details will be published with the price.
